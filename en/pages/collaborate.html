Calls for collaboration

My research is at a stage where specific external expertise would meaningfully advance the program. I am actively looking for collaborators in two domains.

Lambda calculus specialist

Current formal semantics uses lambda notation as a notational convenience, but its theoretical backend remains set-theoretic. Functions are modeled as sets of ordered pairs, and the procedural logic of functions-as-rules is not taken into account. I am developing a foundational shift: adopting pure lambda calculus — where functions are rules of correspondence, not sets — as the central framework for formal semantics. This is not a notational adjustment; it is a paradigm shift with consequences for how we model quantification, definiteness, plurality, and ambiguity. I am looking for a lambda calculus specialist to collaborate on the formal rigor of this program, with a view to joint publications.

Psycholinguist — plurality & quantification

I am developing an ambiguity-based theory of plurality that treats the collective vs. distributive distinction as reflecting two primitive and equally basic notions of plurality — conjunctive plurality and scattered plurality. This hypothesis makes empirical predictions about how speakers process plural expressions, and I am looking for a psycholinguist working at the linguistics-cognition interface to bring experimental evidence to bear on these predictions. Joint publications are the expected outcome.

Full five-year research program (2026–2031)

Overview

This five-year program pursues a unified agenda at the intersection of formal semantic theory and empirical fieldwork on Ivorian languages. It is organized around three theoretical programs — neo-componential semantics, a theory of ambiguity, and minimalist semantics — and a systematic fieldwork component designed both to feed current research and to open new empirical fronts. The program is explicitly collaborative: it welcomes partnerships with formal semanticists, lambda calculus specialists, and psycholinguists working on the relationship between linguistic theory and cognition.

Theoretical priorities

Neo-componential semantics — Consolidating the foundational formalism and refining the method for identifying semantic features. The longer-term goal is to extend the framework to the modeling of complex meanings and their interactions, with ambiguity as a central test case.

Theory of ambiguity — Working toward a rigorous formal model of linguistic ambiguity — one that treats ambiguity as a structural property of language rather than a residue to be eliminated. A framework-independent contribution: formal tools and empirical diagnostics applicable across theoretical frameworks.

Minimalist semantics — Advancing on two fronts simultaneously: resolving key open questions around binding, movement, and displaced constituents under the copy theory of movement; and applying the emerging theory to language data in real time through MA and PhD supervision.

Fieldwork priorities

  • Data on ambiguities, semantic effects of movement, and pronominal interpretation
  • Data on plurality and universal quantifiers across Ivorian languages
  • Data on definiteness and demonstratives across Ivorian languages
  • Data on TAM systems across Ivorian languages
  • Large corpus data on Wobe — toward a collaborative grammar
  • Design of elicitation materials for domains not yet covered

Expected outputs

Publications are the primary expected output. A monograph is envisaged as a longer-term deliverable. Funding is not currently sought but remains a possibility. Supervision of MA and PhD students on related topics is actively encouraged, subject to institutional and administrative feasibility.
